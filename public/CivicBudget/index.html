The whole budget year

From the chart of accounts to the public portal, in one place.

CivicBudget follows the Ohio budget calendar and hands off to the ERP where the ERP is the system of record. Nothing is re-keyed.

  1. ERPChart from the ERPFunds, departments, and accounts arrive by API or file.

AI assistant

Ask your budget. Let it do the work. Nothing changes until you click.

The assistant sits beside every page. It answers from the same reports the screens show, finds the page you need, checks a budget before it goes to council, and drafts changes: a five-year plan, a raise across lines, a department's narrative. Every change comes back as a card, and only your click makes it.

For the fiscal officer

"Raise utilities 5%." Here is every line it would change.

Ask in plain words. The assistant works the change out with the same code the page uses and shows each line before and after, with the total. Confirm, and the worksheet updates; if someone changed a line in the meantime, nothing is changed and it says which one.

  • "How are actuals compared to our budget so far this year?"
  • "Check my budget before I propose it," then "fix the Street fund"
  • "Build a five-year plan where each year goes up 4%"
  • Every confirmed change is in the audit trail, marked as the assistant's

For residents

A question box that knows only what you published.

On the public portal, anyone can ask about the adopted budget in their own words and get a short answer with a link to the page that shows it. It reads the published budget and nothing else, so it cannot reveal a draft, spending to date, or anything about a person, and it says so when something is not published.

  • No opinions, no predictions: it describes what the budget says
  • Works without JavaScript, like the rest of the portal
  • Its own switch, and a set number of questions a month

It acts as you

Its tools are the app's own services, run as the signed-in user. A department head's assistant sees one department; a Viewer's cannot change anything. The model never touches the database.

Nothing changes until you click

No tool can commit or confirm. A proposal is used once, belongs to your browser tab, and is checked again when you confirm it.

Your choice of model

Claude by default, through a standard interface, so an operator can choose another. Off until a government's Administrator turns it on. Questions are never stored.

Forecasting

See five years ahead, and where this year is really heading.

A budget that balances this year can still run a fund dry in three. CivicBudget carries a multi-year plan with every budget, and paces this year's actuals from the ERP against last year's calendar, so both questions have an answer before council asks them.

Multi-year plan

Change an assumption, watch every fund roll forward.

Set how revenues and spending change each year, type over the years where a project or a grant breaks the pattern, and every fund's balance is worked out to the end of the plan, with the Ohio limit checked in each year.

  • Up to ten years; nothing projected is stored, so it never disagrees with the budget
  • Moves on a year when next year's budget starts
  • Published to residents as an outlook, with the assumptions shown

This year, on pace

Projections that know the seasons.

By August a village has collected nearly all its property tax and two-thirds of little else. So each account is paced by where it stood last year at the same month, not by a straight line, and each fund's year-end balance is projected from that.

  • Budget against actual, revenue against receipts, projected fund balances
  • Encumbrances count as spent
  • Trends across years, and the appropriation measure, from the ERP's books

Department requests

Department heads budget for themselves.

Each department user sees only their own programs: last year's actual, this year's budget, and this year's spending so far from the ERP, beside every line.

  • A written narrative with every request
  • Submit to the fiscal officer; a returned request comes back with a note
  • Salaries and benefits calculated from positions, pay scales, and OPERS and OP&F rates

The fiscal officer's worksheet

The appropriation limit, checked as you type.

Appropriations may not exceed estimated resources, fund by fund. CivicBudget shows each fund's projected ending balance next to the lines that move it, and blocks adoption while any fund is over, or warns, as each government chooses.

Beginning balance + revenues + transfers in ≥ expenditures + transfers out

Public transparency portal

A budget residents can actually read.

Publish the adopted budget and the portal shows where the money comes from and where it goes, fund by fund and department by department, with each department's own narrative. It loads fast, needs no sign-in, and works on any phone.

  • Only what was published; drafts never leak
  • Year over year, a multi-year outlook, search, and spreadsheet downloads
  • Residents can ask it questions in their own words
  • Your seal in the header

Questions

What people ask first.

Does it replace our ERP?

No. The ERP stays the system of record for the ledger, payroll, and purchasing. CivicBudget prepares the budget, sends the adopted budget to the ERP as a journal, and reads actuals back.

What does the AI assistant actually do?

It answers questions about the budget and the ERP's actuals from the same reports the pages show, finds the page you need, reviews a budget before it goes to council, and drafts changes: a multi-year plan, a change across many lines, a fix for a fund over its limit, the budget message, a department's narrative. Each change comes back as a card showing every line before and after, and nothing happens until someone clicks.

Can the assistant see or do more than I can?

No. Its tools are the app's own services, run as the signed-in user, so it sees exactly what that person's account sees and can change only what they could change on the page. It never touches the database. A government's Administrator decides whether it is on at all.

Which AI model does it use?

Anthropic's Claude by default, through a standard interface, so an operator can choose another provider. The live demo runs GLM 5.3 Flash, a small, fast model, so an answer takes a few seconds and can be wrong; the linked pages always hold the official figures.

What can the public question box say?

Only what the portal publishes. It reads the published budget and nothing else, says plainly when something is not published (like spending so far this year, or a draft), gives no opinions, and has limits per visitor and per month.

Which ERPs does it work with?

The demo runs against a simulated ERP. Connecting a real one means writing its adapter, by API or by import file, which is the conversation to have with the vendor.

Is it only for Ohio?

It is built around Ohio's rules: the appropriation limit, the certificate of estimated resources, the Auditor of State's chart of accounts, OPERS and OP&F. Other states would need their own rules added.

Is it SOC 2 certified?

It is designed and built to achieve SOC 2 compliance, with the controls, policies, and evidence documented. A SOC 2 report comes from an independent auditor, and none has been issued yet.
